After the two coders completed their assessments, the codings were gathered for \gls{irr} analysis. \autoref{fig:irr_table_ass} shows the calculated agreement percentage, \gls{irr}-coefficients and prevalence accross each category. \hyperref[envnegl]{ENVNEGL} was not found in any of the transcribt by either coder, so a coefficient could not be calculated. \autoref{fig:alpha_kappa_ass} shows the level of agreement on each category. It was noticed a weak agreement (both coefficients above 0.6) in the categories of
\begin{itemize}
    \item \hyperref[cogpass]{COGPASS}
    \item \hyperref[semunder]{SEMUNDER}
    \item \hyperref[overauto]{OVERAUTO},
\end{itemize}
a systematic disagreement (both coefficients below 0) in the categories of
\begin{itemize}
    \item \hyperref[anthro]{ANTHRO}
    \item \hyperref[realtime]{REALTIME}
    \item \hyperref[contexneg]{CONTEXNEG},
\end{itemize}
a minor systematic disagreement (one coefficient below 0) in the categories of
\begin{itemize}
    \item \hyperref[semunder]{SEMUNDER}
    \item \hyperref[condacc]{CONDACC},
\end{itemize}
and a minor agreement (both coefficients between 0 and 0.6) in the rest of the categories.

The coincidence matrices for each category are shown in \autoref{fig:coincidence_ass}. Here, agreements are shown on the diagonal, while disagreements are shown on the antidiagonal. Notice that all values on the antidiagonal are equal as there are only two coders.

There was no strong or perfect agreement on any of the categories, however the coeffcients are vulnerable to a small dataset. The categories and codings were discussed and it was decided on a final coding matrix to be used for the follow-up interviews. The final coding is shown in \autoref{final_coding_ass}.
